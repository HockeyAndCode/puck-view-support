# Puck View Privacy Policy

**Last updated: October 25, 2026**

Puck View uses your Apple Watch's motion sensors to support heads-up stickhandling practice. The app processes motion on your Watch and paired iPhone. It does not upload your motion data, recordings, players or scores to the developer or a third-party service.

## Motion and calibration data

While a Watch session is active, Puck View measures wrist orientation, rotation rate, gravity and acceleration. Measurements include timestamps, sequence numbers and a session identifier. The app uses these measurements to calibrate and recognize wrist movements at the five training positions. It does not directly measure the puck's location or contact with the stick.

Calibration recordings are saved in the iPhone app's private storage. They include motion samples, requested positions, recording times, setup choices such as Watch wrist and stick hand, calibration rules and results, and unsuccessful or interrupted attempts. Imported recordings are stored there too.

The Watch and iPhone exchange motion measurements, calibration files, recognition results and training messages using Apple's WatchConnectivity framework. This happens between your own paired devices; Puck View does not operate a server that receives them.

## Players, scores and settings

Each player you add is saved on the iPhone with a name, an optional height, their Watch setup, their best Timed Trial score and their Timed Trial history: the date of each set, the spots reached and the time to reach each spot (up to the newest 1,000 sets). Settings such as rest time and Watch vibrations are saved on the iPhone and shared by every player.

## Retention and deletion

Everything stays on the iPhone until you delete it. In the app, **Settings → Your data** offers:

- **Delete recordings:** removes every calibration and recording. Players, their setup, best scores and history stay.
- **Delete all data:** removes every player, best score, history, calibration and recording. Puck View starts again with one player. Your settings stay.

Each asks you to confirm and can't be undone. Deleting the app from **Settings → General → iPhone Storage → Puck View → Delete App** also removes everything. **Offload App** keeps the app's data.

Your device backups may include app data, depending on your Apple settings. Deleting in the app does not remove separately exported files, copies held by recipients or existing backups. Puck View has no cloud storage of its own.

## Exporting and sharing

**Export recordings** creates a file with your saved calibration recordings, including failed attempts, and opens the share sheet. Player names are not included. You choose whether and where to save or share it; nothing is sent automatically. If you send a recording to the developer for support, the developer receives what's in that file.

## Purchases

The drill library can be unlocked with an in-app purchase. Apple processes the payment through the App Store. Puck View never receives your payment details; it asks the App Store only whether the drill library has been bought, to unlock it and to restore it on your other devices.

## HealthKit

Puck View uses HealthKit to run an active hockey workout session on Apple Watch, so wrist-motion updates continue when you lower your wrist. It requests permission to write workouts, but does not save a workout to Health and does not read your Health records, heart rate, calories or other Health measurements. Use **Stop** in the Watch app to end its session, and manage access in Apple's Health or privacy settings.

## Accounts, analytics and tracking

Puck View does not require an account or sign-in. It contains no advertising or analytics services, does not track you across other apps or websites, and does not sell your data. Development builds may keep local diagnostic logs of connection status, timing and errors; these are not uploaded, and the App Store release doesn't write them.

## Children

Puck View is designed for players of all ages; kids should train with an adult nearby. The app collects no personal information from anyone: player names and scores stay on the iPhone.
